How to choose a commercial HVAC optimization solution

The right HVAC optimization solution depends on what is wrong with the building today, what the existing controls can already do and who will take responsibility for the result. A controls repair, better operating schedule or commissioning project may solve the problem. A predictive control platform becomes a relevant option when a compatible building needs continuing adjustments as conditions change.

Compare the proposed intervention before comparing the AI label. You need to know which equipment will be affected, what connection is required, who can approve or reverse a change and how any claimed improvement will be measured.

Start with the problem, not a platform shortlist

Equipment is faulty or a control sequence is incorrect. Start with your maintenance and controls team. Optimization cannot substitute for repairing a failed component or confirming that a sequence works as intended.

Schedules no longer match occupancy. Ask what the existing BMS can do, including its adaptive functions. If a supported schedule or sequence change solves the issue and stays effective, adding another platform may not be necessary.

The team cannot see which issues deserve attention. Fault detection and diagnostics can help identify and prioritize problems. Ask how findings become completed work. Some providers also offer automated corrective control, so the label FDD does not tell you whether a product stops at an alert.

The building needs continuing control adjustments. Compare predictive and supervisory control offerings against competent existing controls. Establish what each system may change and how its decisions are supervised.

The building lacks useful sensing or controllable devices. A controls and sensing upgrade may be the appropriate starting point. New hardware can enable a better solution; it is not inherently a disadvantage.

Six offerings worth understanding

These examples cover different approaches to the purchasing decision. They are not ordered by quality, price or expected savings, and the list is not exhaustive.

BrainBox AI: predictive HVAC control

BrainBox AI describes a process that maps HVAC points, learns building thermal behavior, combines building data with external information and writes adjustments back to equipment. Its AI Control page lists connections through cloud connected thermostats, Niagara Framework, cloud integration and a BACnet gateway.

When to investigate: You have a control optimization problem and want to assess a platform that describes predictive, ongoing HVAC adjustments.

What to ask: Which connection applies to this building? Which points will be read and controlled? What installation, support and local control arrangements are included in the proposal?

Source: BrainBox AI AI Control

R8 Technologies: HVAC optimization with related building services

R8 describes Digital Operator Jenny as an AI solution that integrates with building automation systems and adjusts HVAC operation. Its solutions page also describes Smart Audit, an Analytical Platform and Demand Response as related offerings.

When to investigate: You want to assess HVAC control alongside diagnostics, building performance information or demand flexibility.

What to ask: Which of those services are actually included? How are detected issues assigned and resolved? What support, data access and control permissions does your proposed scope require?

Source: R8 Technologies solutions

Siemens Building X Comfort AI: comfort focused optimization within Building X

Siemens describes Comfort AI as using building and environmental data to predict setpoints and write them back to the building. The page includes comfort monitoring and controls to activate or deactivate optimization at building, floor or room level. Building X connectivity is a prerequisite.

When to investigate: Your decision includes room comfort optimization and the Building X environment, or you are assessing that environment alongside other building applications.

What to ask: Which equipment and product version are supported in your building? What connectivity and implementation services are required? Which existing schedules and control priorities remain authoritative?

Source: Siemens Building X Comfort AI

75F: sensing, controls and cloud intelligence

75F describes a building automation platform combining smart sensors, equipment controllers, thermostat interfaces and cloud based intelligence. Its published architecture includes a central control unit and gateway, plus devices that connect to equipment and collect conditions within spaces.

When to investigate: The project includes modernizing sensing and control infrastructure, rather than only adding an optimization service to an existing data connection.

What to ask: Which devices need to be added or replaced? Which working HVAC equipment can remain? Who installs and maintains the controls, and what happens locally if connectivity is interrupted?

Source: 75F Smart Building Automation

JLL Hank: HVAC control within a building operations context

JLL’s account of Hank at 240 Blackfriars describes integration with the existing BMS, building data sent to cloud infrastructure and equipment digital twins used to anticipate heating and cooling needs. The account also describes the role of JLL’s site and property management teams in implementation.

When to investigate: You want to evaluate control optimization together with the people responsible for operating and supporting the building.

What to ask: Is the described service available for your location and contractual arrangement? Who handles installation, ongoing supervision, operator requests and changes to the building?

This source is a vendor published customer story. It illustrates one implementation, not a guaranteed outcome or a universal service package.

Source: JLL’s Hank account at 240 Blackfriars

CopperTree Analytics Kaizen ASO: diagnostics connected to supervisory control

CopperTree describes Kaizen Automated System Optimization as extending its FDD and energy management platform with control commands. Its published implementation includes FDD data collection, a separate hardware appliance for communication and a review of the target control sequence. The page describes command permissions, priority management, expiration and an action log. It says the current appliance firmware supports native BACnet devices.

When to investigate: You want diagnostics and corrective control in a connected workflow, or already use Kaizen and are considering an additional control scope.

What to ask: Which faults and sequences can be addressed automatically? Which commands are allowed? How are expiration and fallback verified on the actual devices? Which appliance and firmware are included?

Source: CopperTree Kaizen ASO

Where Energia AI fits

Energia AI is an AI control platform for commercial HVAC. It learns a building’s response to weather, occupancy and control changes. We assess existing commercial buildings with central controls and start with a bounded operating issue and a measured pilot.

The useful question is not whether AI, a building model or operator oversight sounds different. Other providers describe those capabilities too. The question is whether the proposed approach fits your equipment, operating problem, control permissions and measurement requirements.

Our simulation work is preparation for that evaluation, not evidence of savings in an occupied building. We do not present a measured field savings result or a comparative performance advantage here. Compatibility, integration work, any additional devices and responsibilities must be established for the actual site.

Ask every provider for the same evidence

  1. An explicit scope. Identify equipment, zones, meters, data sources and permitted commands. Distinguish included services from optional products.
  2. An integration plan. Record existing interfaces, required changes, installation work and the people responsible for support. A shared protocol name is not a compatibility assessment.
  3. Operator authority. Agree who approves changes, what operators can see and how local control is restored. Ask for site verification, not just a diagram of intended behavior.
  4. A defensible comparison. Agree the baseline, measurement boundary and treatment of weather, occupancy and other changes before drawing a savings conclusion.
  5. An operational result. Review comfort and operating requirements alongside energy and cost. A lower bill is not enough if the building no longer meets its requirements.
  6. Complete commercial terms. Include integration, devices, service, support, measurement and exit obligations. Establish who pays the bills and who retains any benefit.

The DOE FEMP measurement guidance distinguishes system level, whole building and calibrated simulation approaches. Those are different ways to establish evidence, not interchangeable labels for a savings dashboard.

Make the next decision smaller

Choose one building and one operating issue. Ask your engineer and existing controls provider what can be resolved now, what evidence is missing and whether a separate optimization pilot is justified. A good evaluation can conclude that a simpler intervention is the better choice.

If you want to assess Energia AI for that building, discuss a measured pilot. Bring the operating concern, available trends and meter data, and the person who can authorize a controls review.
